# Code of Conduct

AGNITE contributors are expected to maintain a respectful, technically rigorous and inclusive project environment.

## Expected behaviour

- communicate respectfully, especially during disagreement;
- critique ideas and code rather than people;
- document assumptions and limitations clearly;
- give credit for data, code, research and design contributions;
- welcome constructive questions from contributors of different experience levels;
- avoid publishing private information, credentials or sensitive user data;
- report model/data results accurately without exaggerating performance.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- harassment, threats, discrimination or personal attacks;
- deliberate misrepresentation of another contributor's work;
- sharing secrets, personal data or private communications without permission;
- knowingly presenting simulated data as live/real data;
- knowingly presenting unsupported model metrics as validated real-world performance;
- disruptive behaviour that prevents productive collaboration.

## Technical integrity

Because AGNITE works with fire/thermal intelligence, technical integrity is part of project conduct.

Contributors should preserve clear distinctions between:

- satellite thermal detections;
- inferred classifications;
- heuristic risk estimates;
- historical recurrence predictions;
- confirmed real-world incidents.

Uncertainty, abstention and data-source limitations should remain visible rather than being removed to make results appear stronger.

## Reporting concerns

Raise ordinary collaboration issues through repository discussions/issues where appropriate. Security-sensitive, privacy-sensitive or personally identifying concerns should be reported privately to repository maintainers.

Maintainers may edit, reject or remove contributions that violate these expectations.
